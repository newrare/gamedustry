Slipdeck — gameplay prototype

One card at a time. Swipe left to bin it, right to keep it. Fill the hand and it is scored as poker: a pair or better pays, high card busts. Discards fill the chute, keeps empty it, and every card burns on a fuse.

What to judge

Is the chute the right cost? This is the whole design. With chute depth high the player never has to keep a bad card and the game is a slow, safe grind; at 2 they are shoved into junk hands every few seconds. Watch the keep % counter — a run that settles around 45–60% means the player is really choosing. Pinned above 70% means the chute is too tight, below 35% means it costs nothing.

Does the hand size fit a playable? Five cards is real poker but a hand takes ~8 s to fill, so a 30 s round is three hands and one bust ruins it. Drop to 3 and the payouts come every 4 s — far more playable-shaped, and the paytable switches with it. Judge on hands/min, not on score.

Does the shoe need to cheat? shoe bias is the chance that a deal is picked to keep the hand's line alive instead of coming off the top. At 0 the run is honest and mostly junk; push it up until the hands feel lucky rather than random. Ad players get 20 seconds — they are supposed to feel good, not tested.

What a scripted pilot already found (9 seeds, a pilot with a tunable THINK time per card): the keep rate settles at 52-55% at every setting — but that reading was wrong, and the shipped build caught it: the pilot treated a one-card hand as being on no line, so it binned the first card of every hand on principle. Fix that and the keep rate jumps to 76-90%: at five cards, keeping is almost always right, the bin gesture is nearly vestigial and the chute never bites. Read any keep rate here with that in mind. The chute never overflows for a player who watches it — it forces them to keep a card they did not want first, which is the point — but a pilot that bins every unhelpful card without looking is dead in 21 s on three overflows, so the pips have to be impossible to miss. Deepening it to 5 makes the game worse (keep drops to 44%, hands fall by a third) because binning freely burns the cards a hand needs. 3 is the floor and the ceiling.

The fuse is invisible until you are slow. A pilot at 0.35 or 0.9 s per card plays an identical run at 2.6, 3.6 or 4.6 s of fuse. But at 2.6 s of thinking the 2.6 s fuse kills the run in 8.6 s — three burnouts, nothing seen. At 3.6 s the same player survives the full 30 s, and 4.6 s buys nothing more. That is why the fuse sits at 3.6: it costs a quick player nothing and stops the deliberate one from being executed for thinking.

The real cost of five cards is tempo. A hand needs five keeps, so at a ~54% keep rate it eats ~9 cards. A deliberate player (0.9 s per card) completes 2.8 hands in a 30 s run — a payoff every ~10 s — against 5.6 for a machine-fast one. shoe bias is the only knob that moves it: 0.45 gives 2.1 hands, 0.70 gives 2.8, 0.90 gives 2.9, so 0.70 is the knee and the default. If two or three payoffs per run is too thin for the campaign, the honest fix is a longer round, not a tighter chute.

Is the fuse pressure or noise? Turn the fuse toggle off and play a run untimed. If the game is better without it, the pressure belongs in the round clock alone and the card should simply wait.

  • Bust = the hand filled up with nothing better than high card. It costs a life and clears the hand.
  • Chute overflow = a discard with the meter already full. Costs a life and empties the chute.
  • Every paying hand adds time bonus seconds to the clock, so a good player extends their own run.
  • The keep preview under the card is the honesty knob: it tells the player what the card would make. Turn it off to see whether the hand is readable on its own.
