Gearball Loop — gameplay prototype

A closed ring of tangent gears, one continuous track along their rims, slots at a constant pitch riding it. Tap to drop a ball on the entry: an empty slot takes it, a full slot destroys it. Everything that decides whether this is a game is a slider below.

What to judge

Is the lead readable? The fall lasts sqrt(2h/g) — about a third of a second — so the slot you must aim at is one or two slots upstream of the hopper. Ghost target draws where the ball will actually land, in green or red. Turn it off: if the game dies without it, the real build needs it (or a slower rim).

Does the circuit breathe? With ball laps at 0 nothing ever leaves and the ring silts up until every drop is a miss. Any value above 0 makes a ball ride that many laps, pay out and pop, which is the sink that keeps free slots coming. Watch the fill counter: a run that hovers around 50–70% is a game, one that pins at 100% is a wall.

Do the gear sizes matter? A ball keeps one rim speed everywhere, so a big gear is a long calm arc and a small one is a fast tight corner. What changes with size is the shape of the track, not the pace — check whether that is enough variety, or whether the next build needs the small gears to actually mean something.

  • Entry is the highest point of the track; the drop column is kept clear of every other gear when the circuit is grown, so a ball never falls through teeth.
  • Parity forces an even gear count: meshing gears counter-rotate, so a closed ring only closes with an even number of them.
  • Consecutive gears alternate the outer and the inner arc of their rim, which is why the track weaves instead of tracing a circle.
