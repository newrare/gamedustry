Dynamic menu

One hero object instead of a village: it turns in a demo, its parts are the doors. Tap a pastille or a part of the machine; drag it to turn it.

The idea

What it replaces. The village is a painted hub and six cut houses, composed per game. Here the menu is the game's own hero — the dozer — turning on the menu screen. Each door of the village is a pastille pinned on a part of it.

  • Play → the blade (what plays). It charges off screen and the round starts.
  • Levels → the tracks (the road). They roll.
  • Leaderboard → the cab. The blade is raised like a cup.
  • Album → the stickers on the hood. They pop.
  • Shop → the exhaust. It revs and spits coins.
  • Daily gift → the beacon. It flashes while a gift waits.
  • Options → the corner control, unless a part is given to it (the ripper).

The sequence is the strip under the phone: the idle demo plays, a tap picks a door, the camera flies to that part while the machine turns to present it, the part plays its animation, then the view rises as a sheet, the part still framed above it. Closing it flies back and the demo resumes.

What it keeps from the village: a door per role and the shell's word for it, the badge as a number, a pulse only while something waits, the wallet band, Options in the corner, Help on the map's level 0.

What it adds: the part answers too (the beacon flashes, the coins come out), and a door can be reached two ways — its pastille or the part itself. A pastille on a part turned away dims, never disappears.

Open questions: how long a player waits between the tap and the view (the beats' total, in the readout), and whether a game whose hero is not a machine (a card, a ball) has parts enough for six doors.
